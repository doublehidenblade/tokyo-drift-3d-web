**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

### Yamanashi Press and Broadcasting Center (Kōfu, 1966 – Kenzo Tange)
* **Height & Storeys:** 58.3 m; 8 storeys above ground, 2 basement levels.
* **Structure & Exposed Concrete:** Steel-reinforced concrete (SRC) megastructure organized around 16 massive cylindrical service cores (5 m diameter) with suspended cantilevered functional volumes; features extensive untreated, exposed board-formed concrete showcasing the imprint of wooden formwork.
* **Window Treatment:** Deep-set ribbon windows alternating with solid concrete parapets; framed primarily with dark anodized bronze/brown-finished aluminum sash profiles.
* **Ground Floor Treatment:** An open, partially permeable public ground plane configured with pilotis between the giant concrete shafts, accommodating vehicular drop-off, circulation paths, and recessed glass-walled entrances and printing-plant viewing areas.

### Shizuoka Press and Broadcasting Center (Tokyo Ginza, 1967 – Kenzo Tange)
* **Height & Storeys:** 57.0 m; 12 storeys above ground (plus a rooftop machinery unit), 1 basement level.
* **Structure & Exposed Concrete:** Steel-reinforced concrete (SRC) cylindrical central core (7.7 m diameter) supporting cantilevered steel-frame office box units; the core utilizes rough-textured, exposed board-formed poured concrete.
* **Window Treatment:** Modular unit glazing consisting of floor-to-ceiling panoramic glass panels and continuous horizontal ribbon-style glazing set in dark bronze-finish / dark-anodized metal frames within steel capsule pods.
* **Ground Floor Treatment:** Drastically reduced structural footprint on a tight triangular site (189 m²); the circular core anchors directly into the pavement, wrapped at grade by recessed glass storefront windows and an entrance foyer.

---

### Tokyo Kotsu Kaikan (Yurakucho, 1965 – Mitsubishi Estate Design)
* **Height & Storeys:** 54.4 m (roof height) / 57.1 m (architectural peak including revolving restaurant superstructure); 15 storeys above ground, 4 basement levels.
* **Structure & Exposed Concrete:** Steel-reinforced concrete (SRC) frame; *not* raw board-formed concrete—the facade is clad primarily in smooth composite panels, precast aggregate, and polished masonry/tile facing typical of commercial urban transport hubs.
* **Window Treatment:** Continuous horizontal ribbon windows wrapping the mid-rise block, fitted with clear, natural-anodized aluminum sash frames.
* **Ground Floor Treatment:** Highly porous, civic transit-oriented commercial podium featuring double-height display glass, direct underground metro/rail pedestrian concourse links, and arcade entries.

---

### Typical Showa Marunouchi Office Slab (Pre-1963 Paradigm)
* **Height Limit & Massing:** Strictly governed by the Building Standard Act's 100-shaku (31 m) height ceiling (lifted in 1963 in favor of FAR/容積率 regulations); formed uniform, flat-roofed, block-filling rectangular monolithic slabs typically rising 8 to 9 storeys.
* **Structural & Facade Materials:** Rigid steel-reinforced concrete (SRC) post-and-beam frame; exteriors universally clad in unglazed ceramic tiles (scratch/split tiles), granite, or cast stone rather than raw brutalist concrete to resist weathering and urban pollution.
* **Window Bands & Ground Plane:** Rigid, highly repetitive punched apertures or continuous ribbon windows utilizing early mill-finish or clear-anodized aluminum sashes (replacing pre-war steel); ground floors featured formal banking/corporate entrances, polished stone arcades, and deeply inset entry portals.
