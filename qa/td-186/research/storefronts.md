**Question.** Typical Showa-era (1955-1985) Japanese small shopfronts on shotengai streets: tobacco shop window counters (tabako-ya with the red enamel tabako sign), ramen shops with noren and red lanterns, kissaten coffee shops with display windows and plastic food samples, izakaya with akachochin red lanterns, pharmacies (yakkyoku), liquor stores (sakaya) - frontage widths, shop heights, fascia sign boards (kanban) sizes and materials, sliding glass doors, the residence above the shop (tenpo heiyo jutaku). Give dimensions.

**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

* **Frontage width (間口, maguchi):** Small independent shops typically spanned 1.5 to 3 *ken* (approx. 2.7 m to 5.5 m), based on traditional 910 mm or 1,820 mm structural modules; micro-scale storefronts (e.g., standalone *tabako-ya*) could measure as narrow as 1 *ken* (approx. 1.8 m).
* **Overall building height (*tenpo heiyō jūtaku*):** Typical two-story wood-frame or light-gauge steel shop-residences stood 6.0 m to 7.5 m tall at the ridge line.
* **Storefront floor-to-ceiling height (1st floor):** Ground-level retail ceiling heights generally measured 2.4 m to 3.0 m, while the upper-floor living quarters had lower residential ceilings of 2.1 m to 2.4 m.
* **Sliding glass doors (*hiki-do*):** Entrance bays commonly featured 2-panel or 4-panel sliding doors standing 1.8 m to 2.0 m tall, with individual door leaf widths between 800 mm and 900 mm (framed in dark-stained wood early in the era, transitioning to silver or bronze anodized aluminum sash in the 1960s–1970s).
* **Fascia signboards (*ranma / kanban*):** Horizontal parapet signs mounted above the entrance canopy typically measured 450 mm to 900 mm in height, extending across the entire frontage width (2.7 m to 5.5 m).
* **Fascia board materials:** Fabricated from painted plywood framing faced with galvanized sheet iron (tole), back-painted ribbed glass, corrugated PVC, or internally illuminated acrylic faceplates with fluorescent tube backlighting (common by the 1970s).
* **Tobacco shop counter window (*tabako-ya*):** Dedicated service windows measured roughly 600 mm to 900 mm wide by 800 mm to 1,000 mm high, set atop an exterior counter ledge 900 mm to 1,000 mm above ground level.
* **Enamel tobacco sign (*hōrō kanban*):** Standard Japan Tobacco and Salt Public Corporation (専売公社) double-sided protruding or wall-mounted porcelain enamel signs measured approximately 400 mm to 450 mm wide by 400 mm to 450 mm high (or circular with a diameter of ~400 mm).
* **Display sample windows (*kissaten* & ramen shops):** Street-facing shallow glass display cases for wax/resin food replicas (*shokuhin sanpuru*) had depths of 300 mm to 500 mm, heights of 800 mm to 1,200 mm, and widths ranging from 900 mm to 1,800 mm, elevated on a tiled plinth 600 mm to 800 mm off the pavement.
* **Fabric curtain dividers (*noren*):** Split-fabric entry curtains at ramen shops and izakaya measured 1,200 mm to 1,800 mm wide by 300 mm to 450 mm high (half-length *han-noren*), hung from a wooden rod mounted at a lintel height of approximately 1.8 m to 1.9 m.
* **Red lanterns (*akachōchin*):** Exterior hanging vinyl or paper lanterns for izakaya and ramen stalls typically measured 240 mm to 300 mm in diameter (9-to-10 *gō* size) and 500 mm to 700 mm in height, hung with the lower edge clearing sidewalk level by 1.9 m to 2.1 m.
* **Pharmacies (*yakkyoku*) & liquor stores (*sakaya*):** Floor-to-ceiling glass display glazing (1.8 m to 2.2 m tall) across 3.6 m to 5.5 m frontages; pharmacies typically featured raised tile bulkheads (300 mm to 450 mm high) with life-sized mascot statues (e.g., Sato Pharmaceutical's *Sato-chan* elephant) standing approx. 700 mm to 1,000 mm tall.
