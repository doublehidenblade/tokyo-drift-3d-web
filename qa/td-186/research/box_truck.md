**Question.** 1970s Japanese 2-ton cab-over trucks with a dry van (panel van / aluminium van, 'バン') body: published length x width x height mm and wheelbase mm for the long-body versions of the 1975-1980 Isuzu Elf (3rd generation, TLD/TLG/KT series), the 1973-1978 Mitsubishi Fuso Canter T200 and the Toyota Dyna U10/U20, including the 'wide' or 'long' 2-ton variants and how far the van box was wider or taller than the cab; also the Japanese vehicle size classes that limit small trucks (4.70 m x 1.70 m x 2.00 m for the small-vehicle 4/5 number class).

**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

* **Japanese Small-Vehicle Limit (4-number / 5-number Class):** Under the Road Vehicles Act (道路運送車両法), the small vehicle class (*小型自動車*, giving a "4-number" plate for freight vehicles or "5-number" for passenger vehicles) strictly caps dimensions at 4,700 mm (length) × 1,700 mm (width) × 2,000 mm (height), with gasoline displacement limited to 2,000 cc.
* **Shift to Standard-Size "1-number" Class:** Any commercial freight vehicle exceeding any one of the 4.70 m × 1.70 m × 2.00 m limits (or exceeding 2,000 cc gasoline displacement) automatically moves to the standard truck classification (*普通貨物自動車*, receiving a "1-number" plate).
* **Impact of Van Bodies on Classification:** Standard flatbed versions of compact 2-ton cab-over trucks routinely fit within the 4-number classification (e.g., standard cab width under 1.70 m, height under 2.00 m). However, installing an enclosed dry van or aluminum box body almost universally increases total height past 2,000 mm, and long/wide variants exceed 4,700 mm in length or 1,700 mm in width, forcing them into the 1-number plate bracket.
* **1975–1980 Isuzu Elf (3rd Gen, TLD/TLG/KT Series) Long-Body Van Dimensions:**
  * Chassis/drop-side long models typically sat on wheelbases of ~2,750 mm to 3,300 mm (long-wheelbase TLD variants commonly used 2,750 mm; extended variants up to 3,300 mm).
  * Long dry van (*アルミバン/パネルバン*) overall dimensions: Length ~5,300–6,000 mm (depending on body builder), Width ~1,700–1,900 mm, Height ~2,400–2,800 mm; exact standardized factory-published total L × W × H for every specific coachbuilder box: **Unknown** (installed by external bodybuilders such as Kitamura, Nippon Fruehauf, etc.).
* **1973–1978 Mitsubishi Fuso Canter (3rd Gen, T200 Series) Long-Body Van Dimensions:**
  * Long-chassis models (e.g., T210/T215 series) used wheelbases of ~2,750 mm (standard long) to 3,100 mm.
  * Dry van complete exterior dimensions: Length ~5,300–5,900 mm, Width ~1,695–1,850 mm, Height ~2,400–2,750 mm; single universal factory-published catalog L × W × H for complete dry-van builds: **Unknown**.
* **1970s Toyota Dyna (U10 / U20 Series) Long-Body Van Dimensions:**
  * 3rd gen U10/U15 (1969–1977) and 4th gen U20/U30 (1977–1984) long-wheelbase chassis featured wheelbases of ~2,800 mm to 3,290 mm (e.g., standard ~2,490 mm vs. long ~2,800 mm or U30/U40 extended wheelbases).
  * Full dry/aluminum van dimensions: Length ~5,300–6,000 mm, Width ~1,690–1,900 mm, Height ~2,500–2,800 mm; unified OEM catalogue L × W × H for third-party converted dry vans: **Unknown** (Route Van factory steel integral body models were 4,690 mm × 1,690 mm × 1,990 mm on standard wheelbase, staying just inside the 4-number limit).
* **Wide-Cab 2-Ton Variants:** While standard cabs were narrowed to ~1,690 mm to stay within the 1.70 m rule, wide-cab variants introduced across these platforms widened the cabin shell to roughly 1,880–1,995 mm, which required immediate 1-number registration regardless of bed type.
* **Cab vs. Dry Van Box Width Difference:**
  * On standard-cab chassis (cab width ~1,680–1,695 mm) with standard-width van bodies, the box remained roughly flush with the cab edges (0 to 15 mm wider per side).
  * With wide/overhang van boxes, the box typically measured 1,800–1,900 mm wide, making the box extend **50 mm to 110 mm wider per side** (100–220 mm total) than the standard cab.
* **Cab vs. Dry Van Box Height Difference:**
  * Standard 1970s truck cab roof height measured approximately 1,950 mm to 2,000 mm from the ground.
  * Typical commercial dry van/aluminum van cargo roofs reached 2,500 mm to 2,850 mm, making the van box **500 mm to 850 mm taller** than the bare cab roof.
