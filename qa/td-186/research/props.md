**Question.** Japanese street objects of the Showa era (1955-1985), with dimensions and dates: the 200-litre steel drum (JIS Z 1600 diameter and height), road curve mirrors (karbu mirror, カーブミラー: mirror diameters, pole colour and height, when introduced in Japan), pneumatic tube systems (気送管) in Japanese post offices, banks and department stores (tube and carrier sizes, years in use), mooring bollards on Yokohama piers, the wooden fish boxes (tobako) of Tsukiji market, two-wheeled hand trucks, drum-can stoves (ドラム缶ストーブ) used by workers, and stacked-tire barriers.

**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

* **200-Litre Steel Drum (JIS Z 1600 / Z 1601):** Standard Japanese industrial steel drums have an internal diameter of 566 mm (outer diameter approx. 585 mm) and an overall height of 890 mm. Open-head drums conform to JIS Z 1600, while tight-head drums conform to JIS Z 1601.

* **Road Curve Mirrors (カーブミラー, *Dōro Hanshakyō*):** First developed and installed in Japan in the 1960s (notably by Nobumasa Industry around 1963–1965, with standards formalized by the Ministry of Construction in the 1970s). Standard circular mirror diameters are 600 mm, 800 mm, and 1,000 mm; rectangular versions are 450 × 600 mm and 600 × 800 mm. The support pole is finished in high-visibility orange (76.3 mm standard outer diameter) and stands at a total height of 3,600 mm to 4,000 mm (with mirror center height approx. 2.5–3.0 m above ground level).

* **Pneumatic Tube Systems (気送管 / *Air Shooter*):** Heavily utilized in Japanese department stores (e.g., Mitsukoshi), post offices, and banks from the mid-1950s through the late 1980s (trademarked in Japan as *Air Shooter* in 1953). Standard tube bores were typically 65 mm to 110 mm inner diameter, housing cylindrical shuttle carriers of 50–90 mm diameter and 200–300 mm length.

* **Mooring Bollards on Yokohama Piers:** Standard cast steel/iron bitts (*keisenchū*) along Showa-era Yokohama wharves (such as Yamashita, Osanbashi, and Honmoku) conform to Japanese port civil engineering standards for 15-ton to 100-ton tractive capacities, typically measuring 600 mm to 1,000 mm in height and 300 mm to 600 mm in post diameter. 

* **Tsukiji Market Wooden Fish Boxes (*Toro-bako* / *Tobako*):** Pre-1975 wholesale wooden shipping crates commonly measured approximately 600–650 mm in length, 350–400 mm in width, and 150–180 mm in depth (holding roughly 10 *kan* / ~37.5 kg of fish on ice); largely phased out and replaced by white expanded polystyrene (EPS) boxes between 1972 and the late 1970s.

* **Two-Wheeled Hand Trucks (*Nirin Unpansha* / *Nekoguruma* / *Rearcar*):** Heavy-duty steel hand-pulled carts used in markets and rail depots featured loading beds typically measuring 900–1,200 mm long by 600–800 mm wide, rolling on two spoked or solid wheels with outer diameters of 500–650 mm (20–26 inches).

* **Drum-Can Stoves (ドラム缶ストーブ):** Improvised outdoor work heaters made by field-modifying standard 200 L steel drums (566 mm diameter × 890 mm height) into wood/scrap-burning stoves, retrofitted with an upper cutout chimney flue (typically 100–120 mm / 3.5–4 sun diameter) and a hinged lower firebox door.

* **Stacked-Tire Barriers:** Placed around Japanese construction sites, go-kart tracks, and driving courses; built from decommissioned radial passenger and light truck tires (individual tire outer diameters typically 600–750 mm, tread widths 150–205 mm), stacked horizontally or bolted in columns 3 to 5 tiers high (total barrier height approx. 600–1,000 mm).
