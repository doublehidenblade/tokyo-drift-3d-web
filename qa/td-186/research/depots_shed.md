**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

* **JNR Standard Platform Heights Above Rail:** Showa-era JNR standards set conventional high-level freight platforms (*kōshō hōmu*) at **0.96 m (960 mm)** above the top of rail for general freight, and **1.02 m to 1.03 m (1,020–1,030 mm)** specifically for dedicated boxcar (*wamunyu*) small-consignment handling.
* **Freight Shed Architecture & Framing:** Sheds constructed or expanded during the Showa era utilized steel-truss framing or reinforced concrete (RC) alongside traditional timber and brick/masonry fire-retardant exterior curtain walls.
* **Gable Roof Profiles:** Goods sheds typically featured shallow double-pitch gable roofs (pitch ratios around 3:10 to 4:10) clad in corrugated steel or slate sheeting, often incorporating raised clerestories or monitor roof vents for smoke and light dispersal.
* **Sliding Freight Doors:** Platform-facing tracksides were fitted with heavy external top-hung timber or corrugated steel sliding doors (*hiki-do*), with clear opening widths typically spanning **1.8 m to 3.0 m** and heights between **2.4 m and 3.0 m** (specific variations between terminal sheds: unknown).
* **Canopy Cantilever Projections:** Track-side and cart-side protective canopies were built using steel or timber cantilevered brackets projecting **1.5 m to 3.5 m** beyond the building footprint to provide rain protection over the wagon doorways without fouling track clearance.
* **Shiodome Freight Station — Dates:** Re-designated as a dedicated freight terminal on **December 20, 1914** (initially opened as the original Shimbashi passenger terminus in 1872); freight operations were formally discontinued on **November 1, 1986** (site entirely closed in 1987).
* **Shiodome Freight Station — Shed Dimensions:** Occupied a site of approximately **30.7 hectares** (307,000 m²); individual Showa goods shed lengths spanned up to **100 m to 200 m** with shed depths commonly between **12 m and 25 m** (individual shed-by-shed dimensions: unknown).
* **Akihabara Goods Station — Dates:** Freight handling opened on **November 1, 1890**; elevated freight facilities and wholesale connections were upgraded in **1928–1932**, and all rail freight handling closed permanently on **February 1, 1975**.
* **Akihabara Goods Station — Elevated Structure & Dimensions:** Unique among Tokyo depots for operating elevated concrete-viaduct platforms and steel-framed sheds directly connected to the Kanda Produce Market; total terminal area spanned over **30,000 m²** (3.0 hectares), while precise single-shed lengths and cantilever spans remain unknown.
* **Sumidagawa Freight Station — Dates:** Opened for freight traffic on **December 25, 1896**; unlike Shiodome and Akihabara, it was heavily reconstructed during the mid-to-late Showa era and **remains in active service** under JR Freight (closure date: none / not applicable).
* **Sumidagawa Freight Station — Dimensions:** Operates over a footprint of roughly **18 hectares** (180,000 m²); modernised Showa sheds and canopied cross-dock transshipment platforms reached lengths exceeding **300 m to 400 m** to accommodate full container/wagon block consists (exact historical Showa timber-shed dimensions: unknown).
