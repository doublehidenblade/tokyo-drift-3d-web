**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

* **Frontage width**: In Shinjuku Golden Gai, typical individual tenant frontage is exceptionally narrow at roughly 2 to 3 metres (often representing units under 10–15 m² / 3–4.5 tsubo); in standard Kabukicho Showa-era *zakkyo biru*, parcel frontages typically range between 5 and 12 metres.
* **Storey counts**: Golden Gai rowhouses consistently feature 2 storeys (occasionally 3); standard Kabukicho *zakkyo biru* from the 1960s–1980s typically rise between 4 and 8 storeys (often with 1 basement level).
* **Storey height**: Floor-to-floor heights in *zakkyo* structures are compact, typically measuring between 2.7 and 3.2 metres, leaving clear interior ceiling heights of only 2.1 to 2.4 metres (Golden Gai lower/upper levels are even tighter, often around 2.2 to 2.5 metres per floor).
* **Facade materials and colours**: Showa *zakkyo biru* facades commonly use mosaic, glazed, or split-face ceramic tiles (popular in beige, brown, ochre, grey, or charcoal), exposed rough-cast reinforced concrete (RC), and dark anodised aluminium or steel window sashings; Golden Gai facades feature painted mortar, corrugated sheet metal, wood cladding, and wire-mesh glass.
* **Internal floor-by-floor configuration**: Upper floors in Kabukicho multi-tenant buildings typically operate on a single-tenant-per-floor model (or floor plates subdivided into 2 to 4 micro-units of 15–40 m² each), accommodating separate hostess clubs, snack bars, izakayas, or cabaret bars accessed directly via a common elevator lobby or landing.
* **Vertical projecting signboards (*sode kanban*) — size & spacing**: Vertical signs typically measure 0.4 to 0.8 metres in width and can extend vertically from 1.5 metres (individual tenant) to over 10 metres (multi-panel ladder stack running multiple storeys); panels are stacked continuously with tight vertical margins of 0 to 0.3 metres between tenant slots.
* **Signboard mounting and lighting**: Fabricated with welded steel cantilever arms and channel-iron brackets anchored into the reinforced concrete frame; historically illuminated internally via vertical fluorescent tubes behind printed acrylic (*andon* style), bordered externally by exposed, pulsing neon gas tubes or incandescent chase lights.
* **Ground-floor entrances**: Entrance portals are narrow (around 1.2 to 2.5 metres wide), framed by collective tenant directory lightboxes (*annai-ban*) and standing pavement signs (*a-kanban*), opening directly into a straight, dimly lit elevator vestibule or passage.
* **External stairs**: Typically exposed, steep steel-grated or tiled concrete stairs situated either on the street-facing exterior wall or along the side lot line; these served the dual purpose of mandatory secondary fire egress and direct walk-up access to second- and third-floor drinking venues.
* **Named street example**: **Shinjuku Golden Gai (G2-dori)** — Alleys originally developed around 1950–1951 following the postwar clearance of the Shinjuku station black market, establishing its definitive 2-storey drinking quarter pattern through the 1950s–1960s.
* **Named building example**: **Kabukicho Dai-san Kaiko Building (第三海工ビル)** — A quintessential 1960s–1970s multi-tenant Showa *zakkyo* bar structure in Kabukicho 1-chome, housing tiered micro-bars and clubs characteristic of the era.
