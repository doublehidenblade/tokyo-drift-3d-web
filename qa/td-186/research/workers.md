**Question.** Japanese workwear and uniforms 1960s-1980s: dockworkers (stevedores) at Yokohama/Kobe, fish market workers at Tsukiji (rubber aprons, rubber boots, hachimaki), Japanese police officers of the 1970s (uniform colours, caps, white helmets of traffic police, belts), industrial respirators/gas masks of the era. Describe silhouettes, colours, materials.

**Answer (model summary of the grounded search).**

1. **Dockworker (Yokohama/Kobe) Silhouettes:** Heavy-duty, layered, boxy silhouettes dominated the docks; workers wore durable zip/button jackets (*jampa* / blousons) over undershirts, paired with straight work trousers or wide-thighed baggy *nikka-pokka* (knickerbocker-style) pants tucked into boots or wrapped at the ankles.
2. **Dockworker Materials & Colours:** Constructed from heavy cotton drill, canvas, and emerging poly-cotton twill; colours were utilitarian and muted—primarily navy blue (*kon*), steel grey, olive khaki, and indigo, often paired with white or yellow safety helmets made of molded plastic (FRP/ABS).
3. **Tsukiji Market Silhouettes:** Utilitarian and layered for cold, wet conditions; workers wore knee- to ankle-length bib aprons over bulky sweaters, flannel shirts, or rubberized suits, accented by tightly tied headbands (*hachimaki*) around the brow.
4. **Tsukiji Market Aprons:** Heavy-duty, high-chest bib aprons made of waterproof calendered rubber, vinyl (PVC), or tarpaulin-backed rubber; typically dark glossy black, navy, or off-white, designed to resist fish scales, blood, and seawater.
5. **Tsukiji Market Boots (*Nagagutsu*):** Knee-high, heavy-gauge vulcanized rubber boots—predominantly glossy black or dark navy with thick, ridged non-slip rubber soles (often brown or gum rubber) designed to grip slippery wet tiles.
6. **Tsukiji Market *Hachimaki*:** Narrow strips or twisted rolls of thin bleached-white cotton cloth (*tenugui*), occasionally featuring company names or lucky kanji printed in dark blue or black sumi ink.
7. **1970s Police Patrol Uniforms:** Tailored, mid-century formal silhouettes featuring single-breasted four-button jackets with matching straight trousers, paired with light blue dress shirts; the seasonal standard was deep navy blue wool or poly-wool in winter and grey-blue or light grey tropical weave in summer.
8. **1970s Police Caps:** Structured, circular peaked caps (visored service caps) matching the uniform body (navy or dark blue); fitted with black lacquered vinyl visors, chinstraps, and a gold-colored metal Rising Sun (*Asahikage*) police badge centered on the front.
9. **Traffic Police White Helmets & Belts:** Traffic officers and motorcycle units wore glossy white, rounded open-face or full-coverage safety helmets (FRP), complemented by broad white leather (or white vinyl-coated leather) equipment belts, shoulder cross-straps (*sam browne* style), and white gaiters/gloves for high visibility.
10. **Police Duty Belts (General Patrol):** Heavy, smooth black leather belts with squared brass or chrome metal buckles, fitted with matching black leather flap-holsters for service revolvers (such as the New Nambu M60), baton frogs, and handcuff pouches.
11. **Industrial Half-Face Respirators:** Compact, dual- or single-canister half-facepieces (notably from domestic safety makers like Shigematsu Works and Koken); molded from natural rubber or early silicone elastomer in matte black, off-white, or brownish-grey.
12. **Industrial Gas Masks (Full-Face) Silhouettes & Materials:** Snug full-face silhouettes with rounded twin glass eyepieces (or single curved panoramic visors toward the 1980s), speaking diaphragms, and screw-on threaded metal canisters; facepieces were made of thick vulcanized black or olive-drab rubber, secured with 5-to-6-point woven elastic head harnesses.
